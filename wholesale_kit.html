1 · The Business Mechanics

Assignment (the default move)

You put a $2M+ house under contract at a discount, then sell your POSITION in the contract to a cash buyer for an an assignment fee of $100k+ at this tier. You never own the house, never touch a loan, never swing a hammer. You control the contract, the buyer pays you to step into your shoes.

Double-close (when to use instead)

At $2M+, buyers and sellers sometimes get uncomfortable seeing your fee on the settlement statement. A double-close (you actually buy, then resell same day to your buyer, usually funded by transactional lending) keeps your spread private. Costs a bit more in fees; worth it when the gap is large or the parties are touchy.

The three pipelines that feed every deal

  • Sellers: motivated owners of $2M+ homes (expired listings, probate/estates, divorce, big price cuts, tired landlords)
  • Buyers: 25-50 qualified cash buyers per metro BEFORE you chase sellers — flippers moving upmarket, family offices, international investors, luxury builders wanting teardowns
  • Deal math: know your walk-away number before you sign anything

⚠ Legal note: wholesaling is legal but state rules vary on marketing contracts vs. acting as an unlicensed agent. Assignment language, disclosure, and some states' disclosure/registration rules matter. I'll flag the exact rules for each metro we enter. This kit is business education, not legal advice — heavy contracts get a real estate attorney's once-over ($200-500 one-time, cheaper than a mistake).

2 · Deal Math — The Walk-Away Formula ($100k Fee Floor)

Max Offer = (After-Repair Value × 0.75) − Repair Costs − Your Fee − Buyer's Profit Cushion

The fee floor is $100k. Every deal either clears it or we don't sign it. What that means in practice: you need spreads wide enough to carry a six-figure fee, which comes from three places — deeper discounts from motivated sellers, higher price points (the $3M+ band carries $100k fees far more often), or double-closes where the total spread (not just the assignment) clears six figures.

  • ARV: what the house sells for fully renovated (comps from recent $2M+ sales in the subdivision)
  • Buyers at this tier want to be all-in at 75-80% of ARV including repairs
  • Your fee rides INSIDE that spread — example at scale: ARV $3.5M, repairs $400k, buyer all-in at $2.6M — contract at $2.5M and that's a $100k assignment. Contract at $2.35M and double-close for a $250k spread

Every seller conversation gets this math run BEFORE you negotiate, so you know your ceiling when you hear their number.

4 · Zillow Sweep — Exact Filters (the seller signal source)

Per metro, run these searches. This is human-pace browsing, not scraping — we stay clean.

  • Price-cut hunters: Filter $2M+, sort by "Price Cut (Latest)" — houses that just chopped $150k+ after sitting 90+ days are motivated sellers announcing themselves. Note every one.
  • FSBO: Filter For Sale By Owner, $2M+. No agent to navigate — a direct line to the owner.
  • Pre-foreclosure: Zillow lists these publicly. $2M+ homes in pre-foreclosure are rare but gold.
  • Expired listings: NOT on Zillow (MLS territory). Solve later with a licensed agent partner feeding us expireds for a referral cut.
  • Owner identity: Cross-check address against county assessor records (free, public) for the owner's name and mailing address. Probate check: county probate court records.

Every find goes into the pipeline database (Scarlet logs it). I draft the outreach. You make the call or I send the letter/email.

5 · The Language Kit

Seller Script A — Expired / Stale Listing (price-cut hunter)

"Hi [Name], my name's Ryian. I saw your home on [Street] has been on the market a while — I'm not calling about listing it, I want to be clear about that. I work with private buyers who purchase homes like yours directly, in cash, as-is. No staging, no showings, no waiting on bank approvals. If I brought you an offer with a clean close on your timeline, is that something you'd want to hear about?"

Key: disqualify yourself as an agent IMMEDIATELY. Their guard drops the second they know you're not hunting a commission.

Seller Script B — Executor / Probate / Estate

"Hi [Name], I know this might be a difficult time, so I'll be brief. I work with families settling a property who don't want to stage it, show it, and wait six months for a retail sale. We purchase estate homes directly, as-is, with a clean cash close and no commissions deducted. If handling the house the simple way would take one thing off your plate, I'd be glad to walk you through exactly how it works — and if it's not a fit, I'll tell you that honestly and wish you well."

Key: empathy FIRST, certainty second. Executors are drowning in decisions. You are the one caller offering to REMOVE a task, not add one.

Seller Script C — FSBO

"Hi [Name], I saw you're selling your home yourself — respect, that's not easy in any market. I'm not an agent and I'm not calling to pitch you on one. I work with cash buyers who purchase homes directly from owners. If I could bring you a clean, no-commission offer so you never have to do another open house, would you want five minutes to hear the number?"

Buyer Script (builds the list BEFORE we have a deal)

"Hi [Name], I acquire $2M+ properties in [Metro] — often before they ever hit the market, always at a discount to retail. When I have something in your lane, I call first, and the fee is built in, no games. What are you buying right now — flips, holds, or teardowns — and what does the perfect deal look like for you?"

Ask the qualifier: price band, condition tolerance, area, cash source, close speed. Log every answer. A buyer list with 30 profiles means every contract you sign already has three people who want it.

Negotiation Framing — the big five

  • "What's your best number?" — said calmly, after silence, always after they speak first.
  • Never justify your offer — state it, then stop talking. Luxury sellers negotiate constantly; the first one to fill silence loses money.
  • When asked "what's in it for you": "I'm a principal in the transaction, not an agent — my network of buyers pays me to bring them discounted properties. It costs you nothing; it's between me and the buyer."
  • Handle "I'd rather list it": "You absolutely can, and you'll probably get closer to retail — after 6 months of showings and 5-6% in commissions. I'm offering certainty and speed. If that's not worth a conversation, no hard feelings."
  • Walk-away discipline: the number is set BEFORE the call (Section 2). Sellers at this tier respect someone who knows their math. Chasing kills credibility.

Follow-Up Cadence (same machine as On Desk)

  • Day 3: value touch — a recent $2M+ comp in their neighborhood, no ask
  • Day 7: the honest nudge — "still interested in bringing you a clean offer if the timing's right"
  • Day 14: the closing-the-file note — "I'll take you off my list unless I should check back in spring"

Wealthy sellers move on their timeline, not ours. Cadence = being there when the timeline hits. Persistence Mode applies: no lead gets one touch and dropped.
